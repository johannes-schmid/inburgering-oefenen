# Voice

## Who is reading

Someone who must pass the inburgeringsexamen A2 and is nervous about it. Often: has a job and
children, studies in the evening, has failed a component or fears failing one, and is reading on
a phone. Frequently pays for the exam themselves.

They are **learning Dutch at A2**. That is the single most important constraint on this blog and
the one a generic SEO brief always misses.

## Reading level — hard numbers

For Dutch posts, write at **B1 or below**:
- Sentences **average ≤15 words**; hard ceiling 20. One idea per sentence.
- Paragraphs **max 3 sentences**.
- Prefer the common word: *daarom* not *derhalve*, *ongeveer* not *circa*, *maar* not *echter*,
  *gebruiken* not *hanteren*.
- Active voice. `Je krijgt 15 minuten uitleg` beats `Er wordt 15 minuten uitleg verstrekt`.
- Explain a term the first time it appears: *cesuur (de grens tussen geslaagd en niet geslaagd)*.
- No idioms, no metaphors that need cultural knowledge, no irony that can be read literally.
- Numerals as digits — `25 vragen`, not `vijfentwintig vragen`.

**Address the reader as `je`, not `u`.** DUO uses `u` and we quote DUO verbatim with `u`, but our
own voice is `je`. It is warmer, and it is what a teacher says in a classroom.

For English posts: same discipline, aimed at expats and asylum-status holders whose English is
often also a second language. For Arabic: same, and check the RTL rendering.

## Tone

**A teacher who has seen this exam a hundred times and is not impressed by it.** Calm, specific,
and on the reader's side. The emotional job of most of these posts is to reduce panic — the
reader arrives thinking the exam is a wall, and leaves knowing it is a format they can learn.

- Direct. Answer the question in the first two sentences, then explain.
- Concrete over abstract. Not "goede voorbereiding is belangrijk" but "doe één oefenexamen
  Luisteren met een timer, en kijk daarna welke vragen je fout had."
- Never condescending. The reader is an adult who happens not to speak Dutch fluently yet.
- Never salesy in the body. The CTA does the selling. A post that pitches every third paragraph
  reads as untrustworthy, which is the opposite of the wedge.

## Humour

**Sparing, dry, and never at the reader's expense.** Roughly one light touch per post, usually
aimed at the exam's bureaucracy — never at the difficulty of Dutch, and never at people who
failed.

Fine: *"Je krijgt 15 minuten uitleg voordat het examen begint. Gebruik ze — het is de enige
gratis tijd die je krijgt."*

Not fine: jokes about Dutch pronunciation, about immigrants, about failing, or anything requiring
native-level Dutch to land. Humour that a B1 reader misreads as a real instruction is a bug.

No emoji, in the UI or in article bodies (project rule — lucide icons only).

## Opinions we are willing to hold

Opinionated writing outranks neutral writing, and these are all defensible from `facts.md`:

1. **The exam is a format, not an intelligence test.** Most people who fail, fail because nobody
   showed them the format. This is the through-line of the whole blog.
2. **The numbers circulating online are not trustworthy.** DUO publishes no item count and no raw
   pass norm; the "18 van de 25" figure has no source and competitors contradict each other. We
   say so out loud. See `facts.md` §2.
3. **AI-generated practice material is a bad deal for exam prep.** Competitors generate exercises
   with a model — at least one disclaims accuracy in its own terms. An exam item that is subtly
   wrong teaches you the wrong thing. State the position; don't name and attack competitors.
4. **Three or four practice exams is not enough.** DUO supplies 3–4 per component. Volume is how
   you learn a format.
5. **Timed practice beats untimed practice.** Lezen gives you 65 minutes for 25 questions. Pace is
   the skill being tested as much as comprehension.
6. **Don't sit an exam you haven't practised on a desktop.** DUO's own advice, and their Spreken
   practice exam doesn't even run in Safari.

## Stats worth reusing

All from `facts.md` — check there before using any of them, and wrap in a `FactBox`.

- **Lezen: 25 vragen, 65 minuten.** ≈2.5 minutes per question, including reading the text.
- **Luisteren: 25 vragen, 45 minuten.** Faster than Lezen and the one people underestimate.
- **Schrijven: 4 opdrachten, 40 minuten, met pen en papier.** The pen-and-paper detail surprises
  almost everyone.
- **Spreken: 16 vragen, 35 minuten.**
- **15 minuten uitleg** before every exam, on top of the exam time. Be there **30 minutes** early.
- **8 weken** for your result, by letter.
- **€50 per onderdeel**, €250 total under Wet 2021 *(volatile — always link DUO)*.
- **First 2 attempts free** for asylum-status holders; extra attempts self-funded, no DUO loan.
- **DUO offers 3–4 practice exams per component. We offer 10.**
- **Schrijven and Spreken are scored by certified human assessors** (Examenreglement Artikel 10) —
  which is exactly why our rubric-based feedback is modelled on a human rubric, not a grader bot.
- **The pass boundary is a cesuur set by the Minister and is not published.**

## Stories and openings

Open with a situation, not a definition. "Het inburgeringsexamen A2 bestaat uit vier onderdelen"
is a fine second sentence and a terrible first one.

Recurring situations that are true to the audience:
- **The clock.** Someone who knew every word but ran out of time on Lezen. Pace is the hidden skill.
- **The pen.** Turning up to Schrijven expecting a keyboard, and getting paper.
- **The 8-week wait.** Sitting the exam and then hearing nothing for two months.
- **The one component.** Passing three and failing one — you only retake the one you failed.
- **The desk clerk.** Luisteren items are everyday encounters: a doctor's receptionist, a school
  letter, a delayed train. The exam is about ordinary life, which is reassuring and worth saying.

**Marieke Schipper**, NT2-docent, 10+ years, is the author of everything on the blog. Her
classroom observation is the source of authority — "waar studenten in mijn les op vastlopen" is a
sentence a competitor cannot write. Use it where it is genuinely true; never invent a specific
anecdote and attribute it to her. Generalised classroom experience: fine. A named student and a
date: not unless she supplied it.
