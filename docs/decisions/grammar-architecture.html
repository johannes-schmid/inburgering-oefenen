Where grammar lives

Thirty-one Dutch grammar rules sat inside the A2 Reading course, where they read as “step 2 of Reading”. They are not a reading skill. This page sets out five ways to place them and what each one costs. Options 1 and 2 are now built — grammar is its own track, weighted per skill. What shipped is at the bottom.

The finding

The mechanism you are describing already exists. It is empty.

concept_onderdelen links every concept to the skills where it matters — exactly the distinction you drew, that grammar weighs more on Writing and Speaking than on two multiple-choice papers. The table is in the database, populated, and says almost nothing.

So the platform currently asserts that the superlative matters as much to a listening question as main-clause word order does to a written e-mail. You know that is false. The software does not, which means remediation from a wrong exam answer cannot act on it.

What the six groups are

The grouping itself is sound and worth keeping: Zinnen bouwen (7 rules) · Werkwoorden & tijd (8) · Soorten werkwoorden (5) · Woorden verbuigen (5) · Verwijzen (4) · Spelling & uitspraak (2).

One rule equals one lesson equals one concept. That unit is what makes every option below possible, and nothing here proposes changing it.

Also found while checking: the database holds 51 Reading lessons, not 53. d1-advertentie and d5-regels were written but never seeded.

How others solve it

Nobody who does this well files grammar under a skill

Four reference points, from the textbook we drew the syllabus from to the two ends of the spectrum — grammar as the whole product, and grammar as something you only ever meet when you get it wrong.

Textbook · NT2

TaalCompleet A2

Organised by theme, not skill. Eight themes, and inside a theme the paragraphs alternate between listening, grammar, reading, writing, speaking. Grammar is its own kind of paragraph with its own icon, repeated cyclically — Herhaling: zinnen maken (1), then (2), then (3) across three themes.

47 of 109 paragraphs are grammar. None belongs to a skill.

Free platform · Dutch

nt2taalmenu.nl

The most-used free NT2 site puts Grammatica in the top-level menu as a peer of Luisteren, Lezen, Schrijven, Spreken, alongside Woordenschat and KNM. A learner picks grammar the same way they pick a skill.

Grammar is a sibling of the four, not a child.

SRS platform · JLPT

Bunpro

The opposite extreme: 800+ grammar points are the entire product, sorted by exam level N5–N1, each level split into ten groups. You do not walk a course — learned points enter a spaced-repetition queue and come back as cloze exercises.

Grammar as a queue, not a course.

Productive skills · Cambridge

Write & Improve

Grammar has no menu entry at all. You write, you get feedback per error, and the error carries the explanation. The learner never chooses a rule; the rule arrives when their own sentence was wrong.

Closest to your instinct about Writing and Speaking.

Five options

What we could do, and what each one actually costs

The 28 lessons stay written and released in every option. What changes is where they are addressed from and what decides their order.

1

Grammar becomes the fifth trackBuilt

One Language rules A2 track, sitting beside the four skills rather than inside Reading, unlocked by owning any A2 module. The lessons do not move; the label stops lying. This is the nt2taalmenu answer, and the textbook's icon system says the same thing.

FixesThe card that reads “step 2 of Reading, 2/28”.
CostPresentation and entitlement. No new content.
Leaves openA writer still gets the same 28 lessons in the same order as a reader.

2

One library, four doorsBuilt

The same track, but filtered and ordered per skill by how heavily the rule weighs there. concept_onderdelen gains a third column — core, recognise, skip. Writing opens on word order, verb spelling and connectives; Listening on stress, numbers and small function words; Reading on signal words and reference words.

FixesYour point, structurally — grammar weighted by the exam that needs it.
CostOne column, one migration, and a teacher re-tagging 31 concepts in admin. Roughly half an hour of her time.
Also unlocksRemediation from a wrong exam answer can finally rank what to study.

3

Grammar as reference, not a course

The textbook's appendix model: a searchable rule library with back-references, which exercises send you to. No progress bar, no “2/28”. Honest about what grammar is for — but we hold 28 full lessons with diagrams and narrated explanations, and a bar with 28 things to tick is exactly what keeps people coming back.

FixesThe framing, cheaply.
CostLow.
RiskThrows away the motivational surface we already paid to build.

4

Grammar as a returning queue

Bunpro's model, and also what the textbook does with its Herhaling paragraphs: once a rule is learned it comes back at intervals as short gap-fill items — inside any course, including a listening lesson. It is the only option that makes a rule stick rather than get ticked.

FixesThe difference between 28 lessons done and 28 rules known.
CostReal engineering: a per-user, per-concept review schedule.
TimingWait for users. An empty queue is a handsome empty system.

5

Grammar arrives from your own mistakeBuild next

The Write & Improve pattern, and we are most of the way there: rubric grading of Writing and Speaking already writes one row per criterion. Link a criterion to a concept and the feedback can say — your sentence Ik ga morgen naar de dokter omdat ik ben ziek : in an omdat clause the verb goes to the end. With a link to the rule.

FixesThe candidate stops having to choose grammar at all.
CostA criterion-to-concept mapping. No new lessons, no new schema of consequence.
NoteStays rubric-driven and teacher-authored. Nothing here is framed as an AI judging the answer.

Recommendation

Options 1 and 2 together, then 5. Four is for when there are users.

Grammar becomes its own track and gets a weight per skill, in one change. Together that is less work than a single one of the three courses we wrote last week, and it is the honest picture: four exam courses plus one rule library all four of them draw on, each from its own angle.

Naming

“Grammatica” is a school word with a school feeling for an A2 reader. Taalregels or Zinnen en woorden reads lighter and is just as true. Your call — it is copy, and copy about a real product.

Who pays for the fifth track

Recommended: unlocked by any A2 module, not sold separately. Grammar is not a fifth exam, and pricing it as a product would make the platform look like it is charging for the textbook. There is deliberately no all-access tier to hang it off.

Whether the tiering above is right

19 / 10 / 2 is my draft from the exam formats, not a teacher's judgement. If Marieke moves five rules between columns, the whole of option 2 changes shape — so this decision comes before the migration, not after.

Not on the table

Re-authoring grammar per skill. That was already decided, and the textbook confirms it: those 47 paragraphs are 31 rules with repetitions, not four parallel courses.

What shipped

Grammar is a track, and the library has four doors

Built on 9 September. The lessons did not move in the database — they still live in block B of (a2, Reading), because lesson_blocks.onderdeel is a foreign key to skills.slug and a fifth slug would widen SKILLS, which is what priceForSelection reads to decide a basket is a whole level. What changed is where they are addressed from.

Option 1

The fifth track

/dashboard/[level]/taalregels, in the sidebar beside the four skills and as a card on the level screen. Unlocked by any module at that level, never sold separately.

Grammar is no longer step 2 of Reading.

The refinement

Core rules sit inside step 2

Every course’s step 2 now carries one module — Rules for Reading, Rules for Writing — holding exactly the rules that are core for that exam: 7 lessons for Reading, 18 for Writing and Speaking. For Reading that module is step 2. The full library sits below as its own card, so the minimum route and the look-it-up route are separate answers to separate questions.

Reading: “Grammar 2 / 28” → “Rules for Reading 0 / 7”.

Option 2

The weight column

concept_onderdelen.weight is kern or herkennen per (rule, skill). The library sorts core-heavy groups first and each card says how many of its rules are core for the skill you came in through.

Reading 7 · Listening 5 · Writing 19 · Speaking 21

Also fixed

Found on the way

The seeder un-released content

Adding two lessons required a re-seed, and that upsert wrote review_status: 'pending' onto rows that already existed — silently taking 51 released lessons and 36 released concepts out of the portal. The seeder now reads the existing status and leaves it alone.

An idempotent seeder is not a safe seeder.

Two rules I would move, and did not

The tiering was shipped exactly as published above, so this page and the database agree. But I think onregelmatige-tegenwoordige-tijd (zijn, hebben, gaan, kunnen) belongs in Core rather than Recognise — you cannot write a sentence without it — and lange-korte-klank probably deserves Core for Writing too, since it governs the f → v plural spelling. Both are a one-line change in concepts-a2.mjs plus a re-seed; the unit test will hold you to whatever number you pick.

Still open

The module URLs underneath still read /lezen/spoor/grammatica/<group> — the same screen with the same rows, addressed by its old path. The track index redirects, so there is one door; the deeper URLs are a known cosmetic wart, not a second source of truth. And the name is still “Taalregels” — if you prefer Zinnen en woorden, that is one translation key in three files.
